## Abstract note

Estimating net ecosystem exchange (NEE) in agricultural ecosystems is essential for quantifying carbon fluxes and supporting carbon neutrality strategies. However, satellite-based NEE products developed at coarse spatial resolutions often exhibit limited applicability to fragmented agricultural landscapes, where mixed-pixel effects and scale mismatch obscure field-scale carbon exchange signals. This limitation is particularly pronounced in South Korea, where rice paddies are widely distributed within complex terrain and exhibit strong seasonal variability driven by intensive management practices. In this study, we developed a high-resolution, grid-based NEE estimation framework for rice paddy areas in South Korea with a daily temporal resolution and a spatial resolution of 500 m. The proposed approach integrates satellite-derived MODIS vegetation indices—normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI), fraction of photosynthetically active radiation (FPAR), and leaf area index (LAI)—with meteorological variables from the Local Data Assimilation and Prediction System (LDAPS) of the Korea Meteorological Administration.
