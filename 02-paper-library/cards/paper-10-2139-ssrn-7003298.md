## Abstract note

Water use efficiency (WUE) represents a crucial functional trait that governs ecosystem resilience to climate change; however, accurately partitioning its underlying carbon–water fluxes and identifying the key biophysical drivers remain particularly challenging in water-limited ecosystems. To address this, we developed a new modelling framework that integrates a physically constrained two-layer XGBoost machine learning architecture with a mechanistic soil–plant–atmosphere continuum (SPAC) model. This integrated approach enables robust prediction of gross primary productivity (GPP) and transpiration (Tr) across multiple spatiotemporal scales (half-hourly to decadal) in China&amp;apos;s climatically sensitive Heihe River Basin from 2014 to 2023. Our framework achieves precise quantification of both ecosystem-level water use efficiency (WUEECO = GPP/ET) and plant-level water use efficiency (WUET = GPP/Tr), demonstrating a 19% reduction in GPP estimation uncertainty compared with conventional nighttime partitioning methods.
