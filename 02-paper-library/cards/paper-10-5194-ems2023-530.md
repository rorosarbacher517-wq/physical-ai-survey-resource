## Abstract note

Plant physiological processes like photosynthesis play a key role in the global carbon cycle, thus, having a significant impact on energy exchanges between vegetation and atmosphere. Nevertheless, current approaches to introduce photosynthesis in Land Surface Models (LSMs) are generally based on the extrapolation of locally measured photosynthetic rates to entire biomes. Therefore, plant physiological information at higher spatiotemporal resolution covering large areas (regional to global) is necessary to improve estimates of the seasonal dynamics of photosynthesis in LSMs. Due to the accelerated development of sensors and methods for vegetation monitoring in the last decades the required spatiotemporal resolution can be provided by remote sensing satellite data and derived products. In this context, compared to reflectance based vegetation indices, information about solar-induced chlorophyll fluorescence (SIF) is particularly interesting because of its direct relationship with photosynthesis.
